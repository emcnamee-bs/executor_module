TRADE: Number of Executive Orders Trump signs during the current weekly period (e.g., Mon-Sun).
SETTLES ON: Resolved by the Federal Register's published signing date for each numbered Executive Order; EOs count even if later revoked or amended.
MOVES THE PRICE:
- Legislative/policy agenda: announced plans for immigration, trade, energy, or regulatory actions often preface a batch of EOs.
- Political calendar: anniversaries, State of the Union, end-of-month pushes, or campaign promises can trigger clusters of signings.
- Crisis response: natural disasters, national emergencies, terrorist attacks, or economic shocks often prompt rapid EOs (e.g., tariffs, sanctions, disaster declarations).
- Judicial/legal rulings: court decisions striking down prior orders may prompt revised or replacement EOs.
- Staff/administrative signals: White House press briefings, leaks about draft orders, or Cabinet confirmations hinting at upcoming signings.
- Historical pace: weeks with holidays or travel (e.g., G7, UN General Assembly) tend to have fewer signings; weeks after major speeches tend to have more.
- Congressional gridlock: when legislation stalls, Trump may use EOs to bypass Congress on priority issues.
SETTLEMENT-SENSITIVE FACTS: Only formally numbered "Executive Order" documents count, not proclamations, memoranda, or National Security Directives; Federal Register publication date (not signing date) can lag, occasionally shifting an EO into a later week's count; revoked or superseded EOs still count toward the total; late-posted EOs can retroactively move the expiration or resolution window.
IGNORE: Presidential proclamations and memoranda, agency rulemaking or regulations, executive actions not formally titled "Executive Order," signing statements on bills, EOs from previous administrations, discussion of EO content/policy impact unless signaling pace of signing.
