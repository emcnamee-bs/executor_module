TRADE: WTI crude oil futures settlement price (near-month, e.g. Nov 2026 contract) on Oct 9, 2026, vs strike prices.
SETTLES ON: CME/NYMEX daily settlement price for the active WTI contract (rolls 2 business days before expiry), rounded to 2 decimals.
MOVES THE PRICE:
- Supply: OPEC+ quota changes, US shale output/rig counts, Russian/Iranian/Venezuelan export disruptions, pipeline outages, strategic reserve releases or purchases.
- Demand: China/India industrial activity, global PMI/manufacturing data, gasoline/diesel demand trends, recession fears, EV adoption news.
- Scheduled releases: weekly EIA crude inventories (Wed), API inventory data (Tue), EIA Short-Term Energy Outlook, OPEC monthly report, Baker Hughes rig count (Fri).
- Policy: Fed rate decisions affecting dollar strength, US sanctions on oil producers, drilling permit policy, SPR refill announcements, carbon/emissions regulation.
- Geopolitics: Middle East conflict (Strait of Hormuz, Israel-Iran tensions), Russia-Ukraine war developments, OPEC+ meeting outcomes, shipping attacks in Red Sea.
- Weather: hurricanes disrupting Gulf of Mexico production/refining, extreme cold boosting heating demand, refinery outages from storms.
- Macro/dollar: USD strength/weakness, inflation data, bond yields, equity market risk sentiment spillover into commodities.
SETTLEMENT-SENSITIVE FACTS: Settlement is the official daily close on CME/NYMEX, not intraday spot price, so late-day moves matter most, contract rolls can cause price discontinuities near expiry dates, Brent-WTI spread moves don't directly translate 1:1, holiday/weekend news gaps get priced in at next settlement.
IGNORE: Brent crude price alone (different benchmark, only indirectly correlated), natural gas prices, gasoline retail pump prices, refining margins/crack spreads, unrelated equity market moves, corporate earnings of oil companies without production impact, long-term climate policy with no near-term supply/demand effect.
