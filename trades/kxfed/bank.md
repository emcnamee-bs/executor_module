TRADE: Level of the Fed funds target rate (upper bound) after the Oct 28, 2026 FOMC meeting.
SETTLES ON: Resolves Yes if the upper bound of the target federal funds rate, as published on the Federal Reserve's official website following the Oct 28, 2026 meeting, is above the stated strike; expires shortly after the post-meeting statement release.
MOVES THE PRICE:
- Inflation data: CPI, PCE, core PCE prints above/below expectations, inflation expectations surveys (Michigan, NY Fed)
- Labor market data: nonfarm payrolls, unemployment rate, wage growth, JOLTS job openings, initial jobless claims
- Fed communications: FOMC statement language, dot plot/SEP projections, Powell press conference tone, speeches by Fed governors/regional presidents (hawkish vs dovish)
- Economic growth data: GDP growth, retail sales, ISM manufacturing/services PMI, consumer spending, industrial production
- Market pricing: CME FedWatch futures-implied probabilities, Treasury yield moves, fed funds futures repricing
- Financial stability/credit conditions: bank lending standards, credit spreads, financial stress events, banking sector turmoil
- Fiscal/political factors: government shutdown effects on data releases, White House pressure on Fed independence, Fed chair succession speculation
- External shocks: oil price spikes, geopolitical crises, trade war/tariff escalations, global recession risk, dollar strength/weakness
SETTLEMENT-SENSITIVE FACTS: Only the official published upper bound matters, not market expectations or futures pricing; rate changes happen only at FOMC meetings (8 per year) so news between meetings shifts probability of a move at the Oct 28 meeting rather than the rate itself immediately; a 25bp move is the standard increment, so distribution is concentrated on multiples of 25bp from current level; government shutdowns can delay key data (jobs, CPI) complicating Fed's read ahead of the meeting; dissents within FOMC can signal future path but don't change the current decision.
IGNORE: ECB/BOJ/BOE rate decisions and foreign central bank policy, SOFR/repo market technical operations unrelated to policy rate, corporate earnings reports, individual stock price moves, cryptocurrency price action, state or local economic policy news, Fed balance sheet/QT technical details unless explicitly tied to rate guidance.
