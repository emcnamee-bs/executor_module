TRADE: Whether the US and Iran reach/sign/accept a new nuclear deal (with verifiable nuclear restrictions + sanctions relief) before the deadline.
SETTLES ON: Resolves Yes if US and Iran have agreed to, signed, or accepted a qualifying formal written agreement before the deadline; otherwise No.
MOVES THE PRICE:
- Negotiation status: new talks announced, talks suspended/collapsed, special envoy meetings, Oman/Qatar-mediated rounds, direct vs indirect contacts
- Official statements: Trump, Khamenei, Pezeshkian, Araghchi, Witkoff, State Dept, Iranian Foreign Ministry comments on deal terms
- Nuclear program specifics: enrichment level changes, IAEA inspection access, centrifuge counts, Fordow/Natanz developments, IAEA board resolutions
- Sanctions actions: new US sanctions imposed, sanctions waivers issued, Treasury OFAC designations, snapback of UN sanctions
- Geopolitical shocks: Israeli or US strikes on Iranian nuclear sites, Iranian retaliation, regional war (Gaza, Lebanon, Houthi activity) affecting talks
- Legislative/political moves: US Congress resolutions, Iranian parliament reactions, domestic political pressure in either country
- Third-party involvement: Russia, China, E3 (UK/France/Germany) statements or participation in multilateral framework
SETTLEMENT-SENSITIVE FACTS: Resolution does not require signing to have already occurred if both governments publicly/officially agree to qualifying terms; "verifiable" restrictions require objective means of confirming compliance (e.g., IAEA monitoring); a mere framework, ceasefire, or informal understanding without reciprocal sanctions relief does not qualify; sanctions relief need only cover at least one sanction, not full sanctions removal; multilateral deals count if the US participates.
IGNORE: General Iran-Israel military conflict news unrelated to nuclear negotiations, Iran's missile/drone program or regional proxy activity (Hezbollah, Houthis) absent nuclear deal context, US domestic politics unrelated to Iran policy, Iran's oil exports or economy news not tied to sanctions relief negotiations, past JCPOA historical commentary without new deal implications.
