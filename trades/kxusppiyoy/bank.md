TRADE: Whether US PPI (Producer Price Index, final demand) year-over-year change for September 2026 will be above a given percentage threshold.
SETTLES ON: Official BLS Producer Price Index news release for final demand, September 2026 data, reporting the year-over-year percent change (not seasonally adjusted, 12-month NSA comparison); released mid-October 2026.
MOVES THE PRICE:
- Energy costs: crude oil price spikes/drops, gasoline and diesel wholesale prices, natural gas prices, refinery disruptions, OPEC+ production decisions
- Commodity and input costs: steel, copper, lumber, agricultural commodity prices, fertilizer costs, chemical feedstock prices
- Tariffs and trade policy: new tariffs on imported goods/materials, trade deal changes, Section 301 actions, retaliatory tariffs raising input costs
- Supply chain conditions: shipping rates, freight costs, port congestion, semiconductor shortages, supplier delivery times
- Monetary policy and macro trends: Fed rate decisions, prior CPI/PPI surprises, wage growth data, dollar strength/weakness affecting import prices
- Prior PPI/CPI releases: August 2026 PPI print, core PPI trends, consensus economist forecasts, revisions to prior months
- Demand-side factors: manufacturing PMI readings, industrial production data, housing starts affecting construction material demand, consumer demand strength
- Geopolitical events: wars or sanctions affecting commodity supply (e.g., Russia/Ukraine, Middle East), export bans from major producers
SETTLEMENT-SENSITIVE FACTS: PPI final demand is a headline aggregate including goods, services, and construction, so services inflation (less tied to commodities) can offset goods-price swings; the YoY figure is NSA (not seasonally adjusted) and compares to September 2025 base, so base-effect dynamics (what happened a year ago) matter heavily; BLS may revise prior months' data which shifts the YoY comparison; release is typically delayed if there's a government shutdown or data collection issue; core PPI (ex food/energy) can diverge sharply from headline in any given month.
IGNORE: CPI (consumer price index) reports and commentary, PCE inflation data, employment/jobs reports unrelated to prices, stock market daily moves, company-specific earnings reports, state/local price indices, foreign countries' PPI or inflation data, anecdotal retail price stories without PPI linkage.
