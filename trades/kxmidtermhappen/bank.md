TRADE: Whether the 2026 U.S. House midterm elections occur on time in all 50 states on the federally prescribed Election Day.
SETTLES ON: Resolves YES if every state lawfully accepts ballots in all its congressional districts on Election Day 2026; resolves NO if any single district nationwide has zero ballots accepted anywhere within it that day.
MOVES THE PRICE:
- Legal/constitutional threats: lawsuits seeking to delay or cancel elections, court rulings on election timing, challenges to state election laws
- Executive/legislative action: proposals or statements by the President or Congress to postpone elections, martial law declarations, emergency powers invocations
- Natural disasters: hurricanes, earthquakes, wildfires, or severe storms affecting entire states or large regions near Election Day
- Civil unrest or security threats: widespread violence, terrorism threats, insurrection concerns, National Guard deployments affecting voting access
- Infrastructure/cyber failures: large-scale power grid failures, cyberattacks on election systems, widespread voting machine failures across entire states
- Public health emergencies: pandemic resurgence, quarantine orders affecting entire states
- Political rhetoric: statements from officials questioning election legitimacy or timing, calls to delay from prominent figures
SETTLEMENT-SENSITIVE FACTS: Only a total district-wide failure to accept any ballots (even one lawful ballot suffices) causes NO, localized polling place issues or machine malfunctions do not count against YES, mail/absentee/provisional ballots all count as lawful acceptance so disruptions to in-person voting alone are insufficient, historically U.S. federal elections have never been postponed or canceled nationwide, Election Day date is set by federal law and changing it would require an act of Congress.
IGNORE: Which party wins the House majority, individual candidate races or polling, voter turnout levels, redistricting or gerrymandering disputes, election fraud allegations about vote counting (as opposed to ballot acceptance), early voting or primary election schedules, state-level non-federal elections happening same day, campaign finance or advertising news.
