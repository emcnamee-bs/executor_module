TRADE: Whether the BLS-reported seasonally adjusted U-3 unemployment rate for October 2026 is above a given threshold (e.g., 4.5%, 5.0%).
SETTLES ON: The U-3 rate in the BLS Employment Situation Report covering October 2026, released on the first Friday of November 2026; only the headline seasonally adjusted rate counts, not U-6 or unadjusted figures.
MOVES THE PRICE:
- Labor demand data: payroll job gains/losses, JOLTS job openings and hires, ADP private payrolls, weekly initial and continuing jobless claims
- Labor supply/participation: labor force participation rate changes, discouraged worker reentry, immigration flows affecting labor force size, retirements
- Monetary policy: Fed rate hikes/cuts, FOMC statements on labor market slack, forward guidance on employment mandate
- Macro shocks: recession signals, GDP contraction, stock market crashes, credit tightening, bank failures, corporate layoff announcements (tech, manufacturing, retail)
- Government/fiscal actions: government shutdowns furloughing workers, federal hiring freezes or layoffs, stimulus or infrastructure spending creating jobs, minimum wage changes
- Geopolitics/trade: tariffs causing factory layoffs, trade war escalation, supply chain disruptions, war-driven energy price spikes raising costs and triggering layoffs
- Weather/seasonal disruptions: hurricanes, strikes, or natural disasters temporarily displacing workers (BLS adjusts but surveys can be affected)
- Industry-specific trends: tech sector layoffs, automation/AI displacing jobs, housing/construction slowdown, healthcare or government hiring surges
SETTLEMENT-SENSITIVE FACTS: BLS uses a household survey (CPS) for the unemployment rate and a separate establishment survey for payrolls, so job gains and the unemployment rate can diverge; data is seasonally adjusted so raw seasonal layoffs don't directly move it; prior months are often revised, which can shift the narrative but not this specific print; a government shutdown can delay or alter the release schedule; rounding to one decimal matters near threshold boundaries.
IGNORE: State-level or city-level unemployment rates, U-6 broader unemployment measure, unadjusted (non-seasonally-adjusted) rate, foreign countries' unemployment statistics, stock market daily moves unrelated to labor data, consumer confidence surveys without direct labor market linkage.”}
