TRADE: Will Arizona adopt new, qualifying congressional district maps (enacted, unchallenged, in effect) before Nov 1, 2026?
SETTLES ON: Resolution based on whether Arizona's legislature/redistricting authority formally adopts new congressional maps that survive legal challenge and are used in the next federal election, per Kalshi rules text, by the Expiration Date.
MOVES THE PRICE:
- Legislative action: bill introductions, committee votes, floor votes, map-drawing sessions, legislative calendar/session deadlines
- Redistricting commission activity: Arizona Independent Redistricting Commission meetings, map proposals, public comment periods, commission votes
- Litigation: lawsuits filed challenging maps, court rulings enjoining or upholding maps, state supreme court or federal court decisions, stays or injunctions
- Political dynamics: partisan control shifts in AZ legislature, governor's veto threats or signature, gerrymandering disputes, national redistricting wars (e.g., Texas/California triggering reciprocal moves)
- Federal/DOJ involvement: Voting Rights Act challenges, DOJ preclearance-type actions, federal court intervention
- Election calendar pressure: filing deadlines for 2026 midterm candidates, primary election scheduling tied to map finalization
- Comparable state actions: other states' redistricting moves (Texas, Missouri, Ohio) increasing or decreasing pressure on Arizona to act
SETTLEMENT-SENSITIVE FACTS: Arizona uses an independent redistricting commission, not direct legislative map-drawing, so legislative adoption requires unusual bypass or commission-legislature interplay; temporary stays don't count against YES if maps ultimately take effect; a map fully redrawn by a court or special master resolves NO even if legislature initially acted; withdrawal before implementation resolves NO; must be in effect for the first scheduled federal election after adoption.
IGNORE: State legislative (non-congressional) district maps, redistricting news from other states not named in this series, general gerrymandering commentary without concrete AZ legislative/commission action, census or population data releases not tied to an actual map proposal, unrelated Arizona political news (governor race, ballot measures) with no redistricting connection.
