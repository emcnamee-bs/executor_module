TRADE: Whether the VoteHub 2026 generic ballot margin (D minus R, time-weighted) is above 8.7% on Oct 16, 2026.
SETTLES ON: VoteHub's time-weighted average of generic congressional ballot polls at 10:00 AM ET on Oct 16, 2026; resolves Yes if margin > 8.7%.
MOVES THE PRICE:
- New polling: release of new generic ballot polls (e.g., Quinnipiac, YouGov, Economist, Marist, Morning Consult) showing shifts in D vs R support.
- Political scandals or controversies: resignations, indictments, ethics investigations involving party leaders or members of Congress.
- Economic news: inflation reports, jobs numbers, stock market swings, gas prices, consumer sentiment surveys affecting incumbent party approval.
- Legislative/policy actions: major bills passed or failed (healthcare, immigration, budget, shutdown), executive orders, Supreme Court rulings.
- Geopolitical events: wars, foreign policy crises, terrorist attacks that shift public mood toward one party.
- Campaign events: debates, primary results, candidate announcements, fundraising reports, high-profile endorsements.
- Presidential approval shifts: movements in presidential approval ratings often correlate with generic ballot changes.
- Media narratives: major news cycles (e.g., coverage of a party's internal divisions, unity messaging) that shape voter sentiment.
SETTLEMENT-SENSITIVE FACTS: VoteHub aggregates and time-weights multiple pollsters so a single poll has limited impact, more recent polls weigh more heavily, methodology differences between pollsters (likely voter vs registered voter) can shift aggregate even without real opinion change, aggregator updates may lag actual poll releases by hours or days, house effects of certain pollsters can skew average temporarily.
IGNORE: Individual state or local election polling not tied to generic congressional ballot, presidential approval polls unless explicitly tied to generic ballot questions, historical generic ballot trends from previous cycles, unrelated sports or entertainment "ballot" or "vote" news, local ballot measures or referenda unrelated to congressional elections.
